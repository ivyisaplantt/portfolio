About the Paper

    
The final artifact I chose to include is a research paper I contributed to as a research intern under Dr. Michel Cukier at the University of Maryland. My work focused on automating malware analysis in virtual machines and evaluating how effectively different antivirus (AV) products detect malicious files. We developed a framework to test malware samples in isolated Windows environments and record when each product detected a threat. Using a shared dataset of 2,000 malware samples, we compared AVG, Norton, and Bitdefender based on their detection rates, detection stages, and time to detection. Our findings showed that detection rates alone don't tell the whole story, as products can differ significantly in when and how quickly they identify threats.

    
I included this paper because it represents one of my most substantial technical research experiences. Through this work, I gained experience developing automation scripts, working with virtual machines, designing repeatable experiments, and analyzing data. One of my biggest takeaways was learning how much information can be overlooked when looking at data in aggregate. This experience strengthened my interest in systems, data analysis, and cybersecurity while teaching me to approach problems methodically and use data to support conclusions. It also gave me the opportunity to collaborate on research and contribute to a formal publication.
